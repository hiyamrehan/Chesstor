\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
Physical chess offers no live digital record or engine feedback unless players re-enter moves by hand or buy
expensive sensor boards. Chesstor is a smartphone-based system that watches a physical game through a camera and
maintains a trustworthy digital game state. Recognising a board in a photograph and calling a chess engine are
established technologies; the project's contribution is the harder problem of \emph{keeping} the digital state
correct over time when observations are noisy, occluded by hands, or momentarily contradictory. We combine
temporal confirmation of observations with chess-rule constraints: a visual change is committed only if it is
stable across a window of frames and is explained by a legal move sequence with sufficient margin over all
alternatives; otherwise the trusted state is preserved and the system waits, becomes uncertain, or asks for
manual correction. At the FYP-1 mid stage we have implemented the core state-reconstruction module (session state
machine, temporal buffer, legal-transition scoring including castling, en passant and promotion, uncertainty and
recovery policy), a classical vision front-end, a baseline/ablation evaluation harness, and a GitHub CI/CD
pipeline. On \emph{simulated} observation streams the proposed method outperforms frame-only, temporal-only and
legal-only baselines, but degrades under persistent per-square errors; these numbers are development evidence, not
final results. Real-board data collection, the learned recogniser, the Flutter mobile application and
on-device profiling are planned for FYP-2.
