\chapter{Testing and Early Validation}
\section{Testing Strategy}
Testing (does the code meet requirements?), evaluation (is the method better than baselines?) and acceptance (can
users do the task?) are separated. This chapter covers the first two on simulated data; acceptance testing is planned.

\section{Test Levels and Techniques}
Unit tests (hypotheses, geometry, state machine, config); scenario/integration tests that replay simulated
streams through the tracker for each use case; a determinism test (same seed gives identical results); a CLI test;
and a frozen-seed regression gate that fails CI if the proposed method stops beating the baselines. Static analysis
by ruff; a coverage gate of 85\%.

\section{Test Results}\label{sec:results}
All 60 Python tests, 8 frontend unit tests and 2 end-to-end tests pass. The end-to-end tests render the real app, run
against the real API with Stockfish, and drive guest login, live tracking on the simulated feed, and PGN review through the UI.
API tests also cover the camera pipeline on synthetic phone views, including a board seen from the black side. \textbf{Evaluation (simulated, held-out seeds, frozen configuration).} Latency is measured from the
moment the piece is placed. Each condition uses 10 random games of 30 plies or 18 curated special-move games.
Table~\ref{tab:sim} gives the full results.

\textbf{Observations.} (1) Frame-only B1 is unusable under hand occlusion (move accuracy 0.07--0.19). (2) Temporal
confirmation alone (B2) is already strong when errors are independent, but it cannot represent castling, en passant
(0.81 on special-move games) and degrades sharply under persistent errors (0.30--0.37). (3) Legal constraints alone (B3) fail without
temporal confirmation because they commit during motion. (4) The proposed B4 reaches move accuracy 1.0 in conditions A--E and H (one dim-light game in B still ended with a wrong final board, which we have not yet analysed) and
leads the baselines in F and G, but is \emph{not} robust: under persistent errors it reaches 0.83 and 0.70
move accuracy with 1.4 and 5.1 false confirmations per 100 moves, above our target of $\leq1$.
(5) Interestingly, the no-mask ablation does slightly better than B4 under persistent errors (0.847 vs.\ 0.826;
0.757 vs.\ 0.699) because the simulated mask also discards evidence; it is slower (median 0.67\,s vs.\ 0.20\,s).
We do not yet know if this holds with a real hand detector.

\section{Acceptance Testing / Validation}
Not yet performed. Planned: 8--12 players set up, calibrate, play 10 moves, trigger an occlusion and resolve an
uncertainty (Section~\ref{sec:valplan}).

\section{Edge and Failure Cases}
Illegal physical moves, unresolved conflicts, ambiguous source/destination, missed plies and blurred frames are covered.
Not covered: under-promotion (needs piece classification), a board that is moved or rotated mid-game (LOST\_BOARD is
implemented as a state but has no vision trigger yet), multiple simultaneous hands, and a player correcting a
move mid-way.

\section{Defects and Corrective Actions}
\begin{longtable}{p{0.06\textwidth}p{0.4\textwidth}p{0.4\textwidth}}
\toprule \# & Defect found & Corrective action\\ \midrule
1 & Stability window of 0.5\,s was longer than 8 frames at 15 FPS (0.47\,s): confirmations only happened after a dropped frame stretched the span & Default set to 0.4\,s; lesson: derive time windows from frame rate\\
2 & Simulator noise was independent per frame, so B4 scored 100\% even at 15\% frame error: benchmark saturated & Added persistent-error noise, the realistic failure mode; reported separately\\
3 & Hard ``$\leq1$ unexplained'' gate rejected correct moves under persistent errors (accuracy 0.42) & Replaced by graded margin penalty, tuned on dev seeds only (accuracy $\approx0.70$)\\
4 & Relaxed gate committed one ply before the two-ply recovery hypothesis could be considered & Strict gates tried before relaxed gate\\
5 & Latency anchored at end of hand retreat gave negative values & Anchored at piece placement\\
6 & Found by the API test: the occupancy classifier was correct on every square but only 0.65 confident on a white piece on a light square, below the tracker's 0.70 bar, so no move was ever detected & Occupancy calibrated per square colour with the threshold placed towards the hardest squares; confidence now $\geq0.91$\\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\section{Current Validation Status}
Validated: logic correctness on simulated streams and unit/scenario tests. \textbf{Not validated:} any claim about
real boards, real cameras, real hands, other piece materials, phone latency or users. The simulator is a debugging
and stress tool (dataset D0/D4), not evidence for the final claims.
